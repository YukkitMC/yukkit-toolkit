# Code of Conduct

YukkitMC wants a development community where players, developers, and first-time contributors can participate without harassment.

## Our expectations

Everyone participating in project repositories, discussions, issues, and pull requests is expected to:

- Be respectful, even when disagreeing strongly.
- Focus criticism on ideas, code, and observable behavior rather than on people.
- Welcome newcomers and provide useful, actionable feedback.
- Respect privacy, personal boundaries, and intellectual property.
- Keep reports factual and discussions relevant to the project.
- Follow GitHub's Terms of Service and applicable community rules.

## Unacceptable behavior

We do not tolerate:

- Harassment, threats, discrimination, or targeted insults.
- Hate speech or degrading comments based on personal characteristics.
- Doxxing, sharing private information, or publishing private messages without permission.
- Sexual harassment or unwanted sexual attention.
- Spam, impersonation, malicious submissions, or deliberate disruption.
- Retaliation against someone who reports misconduct in good faith.

## Scope

This policy covers project-owned GitHub spaces and conduct presented as official participation in the YukkitMC project.

## Reporting concerns

For conduct occurring on GitHub, use GitHub's **Report content** controls where appropriate. For project moderation concerns, contact a YukkitMC repository maintainer privately through a channel the organization explicitly makes available. Do not post sensitive identifying information in public issues.

Security vulnerabilities have a separate process: see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Moderation

Maintainers may request edits, hide or remove content, close discussions, restrict participation, or report serious violations to GitHub. Actions depend on context, severity, recurrence, and the safety of affected participants.

Reports should be handled discreetly where practical. Confidentiality cannot be guaranteed when disclosure is required by law or platform policy.

## Good-faith collaboration

Disagreement is normal in engineering. Harassment is not. We want contributors to challenge designs, improve code, and help each other learn while keeping the project welcoming.
